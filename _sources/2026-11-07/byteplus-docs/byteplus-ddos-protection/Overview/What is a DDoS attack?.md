Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) refers to a type of cyber attack where a large number of compromised systems, distributed across various regions, simultaneously send malicious packets to a target server. A DDoS attack aims to block the bandwidth resources of the target server or exhaust its CPU resources, ultimately leading to the paralysis of the attacked server and preventing it from providing normal services.

<span id="215b8915"></span>
## How DDoS attacks work

Attackers exploit malicious software or vulnerabilities to infect devices connected to the Internet and gain control. These infected devices are referred to as "zombie hosts", and the network they form is called a "botnet". Once the botnet is established, the attacker will send remote commands to each zombie host to launch a DDoS attack on the target, rendering the targeted service unable to operate normally.

<span id="f58adcea"></span>
## Potential risks

With the rapid development of attack source obfuscation techniques, DDoS attacks have become more covert and difficult to monitor and defend against. Beyond affecting the target business, DDoS attacks carry potential risks such as data breaches, damage to the corporate brand reputation, and substantial economic losses to the business.

<span id="a332fe52"></span>
## How to identify a DDoS Attack

As DDoS attack methods become increasingly covert, the following signs may indicate that your assets are under attack.


* A sudden surge in network traffic from unknown sources.

* Websites or services suddenly slow down or become unavailable under normal network conditions.

* A sudden increase in server memory or CPU usage.
