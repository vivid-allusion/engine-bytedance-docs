Sorting out requirements needs to be carried out according to the current stage of the enterprise and the actual business goals and scenarios. For example, for enterprises that have not yet established a systematic indicator system or data establishment is not perfect, a complete plan needs to be developed from the establishment of goals; for enterprises with a certain foundation, we need to sort out fragmented information and complex needs, and then merge them into the same growth plan.

To build an effective indicator system, it is recommended to refer to the following steps:

**North Star Metrics Defining Growth → Mapping the User Journey → Sorting through Team Business Scenarios and Core Metrics → Metrics Disassembly**

<span id="_1-定义增长的北极星指标"></span>
# 1. North Star Metrics Defining Growth

As the name suggests, North Star Metric is the company's most important indicator at this stage, guiding all employees in the same direction. The business goals of all teams under the company, even down to the phased tasks of personnel, are to contribute to the improvement of North Star Metric.

<span id="_1-1-北极星指标的作用"></span>
## 1.1 Role of North Star Metrics


* **Guide the way**

   No matter the size of the company, a clear and uniform data metric is required as a guide to ensure that all teams can prioritize tasks, and then effectively focus their firepower and grasp the key points.

* **Increase mobility**

   Setting a data indicator can greatly improve the action force, and also lay the foundation for further connection with customers in the future, accelerating the pace.

* **Guide business insights and experiments**

   Through this data indicator and its segmentation, we can gain insight into the bottlenecks of business growth and form corresponding solutions, such as launching targeted growth experiments to form a closed\-loop growth.


<span id="_1-2-定义北极星指标的标准"></span>
## 1.2 Criteria for Defining North Star Metrics


* **The improvement of metrics can demonstrate that the company is moving in a positive direction.** 

   First of all, the selected North Star Metric needs to fit the current situation of the company/product development, such as the acquisition stage in the startup stage (focusing on new additions), the revenue stage in the mature stage (focusing on revenue conversion), and the saturation stage (focusing on retention). Secondly, North Star Metric should be able to reflect the company's overall operating conditions and trends from a macro perspective, rather than just one\-sided. A typical example is a company with a two\-sided business model, such as ride\-hailing company North Star Metric, which should reflect the balance of supply and demand for drivers and passengers, such as "total rides".

* **It reflects whether users experience the core value of the product.** 

   The core value refers to the pain points that the product solves for users and what needs it meets. For example, for e\-commerce applications, the core value is online shopping, so North Star Metric should be related to user purchase behavior, such as GMV (total amount of goods traded), and customer unit price.

* **Lagging Indicators vs Leading Indicators**

   
   |**Lagging indicator** |**Leading indicator** |
   |---|---|
   |It generally refers to the metrics that measure the results we want to achieve. They're called lagging metrics because by the time we get the data, everything has already happened, so they're always lagging. |Leading indicators are predictive, which means that once a leading indicator changes, we can use this to infer how the lagging indicator will change later. Leading indicators are controllable, and we can rely on our own power to change leading indicators to achieve the lagging indicators we desire. |
   

   For example, some video products may use the monthly membership fee revenue as North Star Metric, which is a lagging indicator because users may not be active despite continuing to pay monthly fees. Therefore, you can consider using a leading indicator such as "Weekly Active Users" as the North Star Metric, so that you can see the problem of the business faster and avoid waiting until the user has actually stopped using the product for a long time and is ready to cancel the subscription.


<span id="_1-3-常见商业模式及北极星指标举例"></span>
## 1.3 Common Business Models and Examples of North Star Metrics


|**Business Model** |**Core value** |**Common North Star Metrics** |
|---|---|---|
|E\-commerce |Discounts and convenient experiences for online shopping |GMV |
|Online Education |The comprehensiveness and convenience of accessing knowledge online |DAU、WAU |
|Community |An effective way to search and disseminate knowledge |total number of questions answered |
|Mobile game |An immersive experience in the virtual world, with fun and achievements that cannot be replaced by reality |game time per person per day |
|Bilateral market |For example, online car\-hailing meets the needs of passengers for convenient travel and provides job opportunities and compensation for drivers |total number of orders |


<span id="_2-绘制用户旅程"></span>
# 2. Map the user journey

After defining North Star Metric, what needs to be considered is how users can step by step achieve the specific behavior represented by "North Star Metric" in the application and experience the core value of the product. Therefore, we can sort out the user journey.

Use the following two examples to illustrate:


* mobile game

   For mobile game products, assuming that its North Star Metric is defined as per capita game time, and the corresponding user behavior is "playing games", then users may need to go through the following steps:

   1. Access to the app

   2. Register an account

   3. Beginner guide on the road

   4. Playing games

   5. Upgrade and continuous customs clearance

* E\-commerce

   For e\-commerce products, assuming that its North Star Metric is set to GMV, and the corresponding user behavior is "pay for the order", then the user may go through the following steps:

   1. Access to the app

   2. Register an account

   3. The first purchase

   4. Repurchase

      Among them, the user's purchase process can be further divided into: click on the advertisement/operation position → browse the product details page → click to buy → submit the order → pay for the order. The segmentation of the user journey is to prepare for the next step of sorting out business scenarios.


<span id="_3-梳理业务场景"></span>
# 3. Sort out the business scenario

The user journey is the backbone of the growth model, and the product, operation, market, and other teams continue to formulate, execute, and iterate strategies on the basis of this journey, so as to promote the team to complete their respective KPIs and improve North Star Metric. Among them, different teams will involve different business scenarios. We can sort out these scenarios in detail, and then build a corresponding indicator system on that basis.

Still taking an e\-commerce product as an example, let's say its North Star Metric is designated as GMV. According to the team, the following common business scenarios can be listed:


|**Team/Function** |**business scenario** |**Scene core metrics** |
|---|---|---|
|Market |Promote and attract new products |Number of new users |
|Product |Registration Process Evaluation<br><br>Search function evaluation<br><br>Product purchase process |New registrations<br><br>Conversion rate<br><br>Number of product buyers |
|Operate |Event operation<br><br>Content operation<br><br>User push operation |Activity conversion rate<br><br>Content views<br><br>Number of re\-activated users |


<span id="_4-指标拆解"></span>
# 4. Indicator dismantling

For the core metrics that each functional team focuses on in a specific scenario, they can be further divided into multiple sub\-metrics and important dimensions to improve the entire indicator system, making it easier to identify growth issues when analyzing data. According to the business scenarios and focus metrics listed above, let's first select one scenario from each team/function as an example to demonstrate:


|**Team function** |**Business scenario** |**Core metrics disassembly** |**Important evaluation dimension** |
|---|---|---|---|
|Market |Promote and attract new products |Number of new users = number of naturally activated users + number of promoted active users<br><br>= number of naturally activated users + number of exposed devices \* click through rate \* activation rate |Promotion channel<br><br>Promotion activity<br><br>Operating system<br><br>Region<br><br>...... |
|Product |Product purchase process |Number of product buyers = number of active users \* registration rate \* product purchase conversion rate<br><br>= active users \* signup page penetration rate \* conversion rate<br><br><br>* Product page penetration rate \* Submit order rate \* Complete Payment Rate |New and existing users<br><br>Registration source<br><br>Operation placement source<br><br>Product category/brand<br><br>Whether a coupon was used<br><br>...... |
|Operate |User recall of a push |Re\-activated users = total push users \* recall ratio<br><br>= Total push users \* push arrival rate \* push open rate \* revisit rate |New and existing users<br><br>Operating system<br><br>Push batch<br><br>Push channel<br><br>Initial channel source<br><br>...... |


A sound indicator system will help ensure the logic and comprehensiveness of the subsequent event tracking scheme design, and ensure that the tracking events and attributes can be used to configure the indicators that different teams pay attention to in different business scenarios to generate business insights.
